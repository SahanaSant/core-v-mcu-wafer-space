## Key findings this session (not yet fully absorbed into ticket prose beyond what's cited above)

1. **Global routing's runtime problem (the originally-diagnosed issue) is genuinely fixed and
   confirmed**: `GRT_OVERFLOW_ITERS: 3` in `flow/config.yaml`, full measurement in
   `flow/clock-frequency-selection.md`.
2. **A second, separate runtime bottleneck in antenna repair/diode insertion** (~2 hours per
   NDR-disable event vs ~25s during global routing) was found and documented in ticket 29, but
   completed on its own without intervention — no fix needed, just a finding for whoever hits it
   next.
3. **Detailed routing shows a real convergence plateau**, not a bug: violations dropped fast
   through iteration ~3 (512K→326K) then flattened hard (iteration 8-10 each gaining under 1%
   while costing 1-2.5 hours). Checked whether `DRT_OPT_ITERS` (the iteration cap, default 64) is
   a safe lever like `GRT_OVERFLOW_ITERS` was — **it is not**: each iteration does genuine
   DRC-fixing work, so capping it just ships more real violations rather than saving free time.
   Violation breakdown is dominated by Short (metal-to-metal) violations, not spacing — a real
   congestion signature. Root-caused (with real evidence, not just "large designs are slow"): real
   placement density is only 26.6% (confirmed from OpenROAD's own global-placement log, well under
   even the repo's conservative 35% target), so this isn't whole-chip overpacking — it's localized
   congestion, most plausibly from where `RepairDesignPostGPL`'s 64,734 emergency buffers (28% of
   the design's ~231K final instances) and the 73 `delaynet_*_clk_PAD` hold-fix NDR nets cluster.
   Checked and ruled out a missing-fanout-constraint bug: `MAX_FANOUT_CONSTRAINT` correctly
   defaults to 10 for this PDK. This looks structural to the RTL's own broadcast/control fanout,
   not a flow misconfiguration — a real fix would be RTL-level (buffering/pipelining broadcast
   signals, or less-flattened synthesis), out of scope for tonight. Not yet turned into a map fog
   entry or new ticket — worth doing if this keeps mattering.
4. **The actual crash cause (host-wide OOM, not container or design) is the single most important
   finding of the session** — see "Live state" above. This should shape how any future long
   `make harden`/`make precheck` run gets scheduled: not casually backgrounded on a host that's
   also running a desktop session and other tools.
